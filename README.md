# Customer Support AI Prompt Playbook

A small collection of AI prompts for everyday customer support work, based on 11+ years in high-volume contact centers. Each prompt is paired with how I handle the same situation with a real customer.

**Privacy rule:** Never paste real customer information into an AI tool. All examples here use made-up details.

---

## 1. Calm a frustrated customer

**Use it when:** A customer is upset about a billing or service problem.

**Prompt:**
> You are a customer support specialist. A customer is frustrated about [describe the issue and the cause, if known]. Write a calm, empathetic reply that acknowledges the problem, explains why it happened, offers to review the account for add-ons or coverage that could be adjusted, and says I will send a written summary of what the next bill will look like. Do not promise anything I can't deliver. Keep it under 120 words.

**Example scenario:** A made-up scenario where a customer is upset because their bill went up after a 12-month promotion ended.

**What the AI gave me:** I ran this scenario with an AI assistant. The reply explained why the bill went up, offered to review streaming services and add-ons, and promised a written summary of the next bill. It sounded like how I talk to customers.

**What I did with the customer:** I reviewed the account with the customer and explained why the bill went up. I went over streaming services and other add-ons that had been added to the bill, and suggested lowering the insurance so the bill landed where the customer felt comfortable. Once we did, I emailed the customer what to expect on the next bill and the bills going forward.

## 2. Keep a customer who wants to cancel

**Use it when:** A customer says they want to cancel.

**Prompt:**
> A customer wants to cancel because [reason]. First, list what I should check on the account to find the real cause, such as ended promotions, missing trade-ins, or new charges. Then suggest 3 respectful ways to resolve the concern once the cause is known, and one question to understand what's wrong. Do not pressure the customer.

**Example scenario:** A made-up scenario where a customer's bill went up $80 after three upgrades, trade-in phones weren't received, and the customer wants to cancel.

**What the AI gave me:** I ran this scenario with an AI assistant. The reply started with what to check on the account (ended promotions, trade-ins not received, new charges), then gave three ways to resolve it and one question to ask the customer. It looked right compared to how I handle these calls.

**What I did with the customer:** I reviewed the account and the notes and saw that no trade-ins had been turned in for the trade-in promotion after the upgrades. I explained to the customer why the bill went up and tracked down the phones. Two had been sent to the store, and one was broken. I got the customer another promotion, told them the original promotions would be added back once the phones were received, and found a promotion for the phone that hadn't been sent in yet. The customer was very satisfied. They had only been upset at first because of the high bill.

## 3. Write an escalation handoff note

**Use it when:** A case needs to go to another team.

**Prompt:**
> Write a handoff note for another team using this information. Include the issue, the customer impact, the steps already taken (including confirming the number with the previous carrier), what I need from them, and the urgency. Also include what I told the customer to expect, any temporary solution I offered, and my follow-up plan, so the next team doesn't repeat or contradict anything.

**Example scenario:** A made-up scenario where a customer ported a landline number to a new cell phone line, but the number won't activate after all tier 1 steps were completed.

**What the AI gave me:** I ran this scenario with an AI assistant. The reply listed the issue, the customer impact, the steps already taken, what I need from tier 3, and the urgency. It also included what the customer was told, the temporary line I offered, and my follow-up plan.

**What I did with the customer:** I followed the full tier 1 process to activate the phone on the number and confirmed with the previous carrier that the number had been ported to us. Since tier 1 couldn't complete it, I handed the case to tier 3 and documented every step in the notes. Before the handoff, I told the customer that porting a landline to a cell phone can take up to a couple of weeks. I offered a callback once tier 3 started, plus a free temporary line, or porting the number onto a new line once it was ready. I told them I would check the account every day until the number could be activated.

---

## What I learned

Being thorough and setting expectations matters most. When customers know the process, they are much less frustrated. Giving them options lets them choose what works while their issue is being fixed. The best prompts follow the same rules, so I add those details instead of leaving the AI to guess.
